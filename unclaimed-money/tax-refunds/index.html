Unclaimed money / Tax refunds

How to claim a tax refund you never filed for

Every year more than a million people who had tax taken out of their pay never file a return, so they never get the refund. For the 2022 tax year alone, the IRS estimated about $1.2 billion went unclaimed, with a typical refund of $686. You get three years to claim it, and then it's gone.

The three-year rule

  • You generally have three years from a return's original due date to file it and get your refund.
  • After that, the money becomes the Treasury's for good.
  • The next deadline: refunds for the 2023 tax year must be claimed by April 15, 2027.

Who's most likely to be owed

  • Students and part-time or seasonal workers who earned too little to have to file.
  • Anyone who had tax withheld from a job they left partway through the year.
  • Lower-income workers who may also qualify for credits such as the Earned Income Tax Credit, which can be worth thousands and is only paid if you file.

How to file a late return

  1. Get the tax forms for the year you missed from IRS.gov. Prior-year forms are there too.
  2. Missing a W-2? Request a free wage and income transcript from the IRS through Get Transcript.
  3. File the return. You can track it with Where's My Refund?

Two catches

  • The IRS can hold an old refund if you haven't filed for later years too.
  • A refund can be used to pay back taxes, child support or other federal debts such as student loans.
