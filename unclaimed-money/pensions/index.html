Unclaimed money / Pensions

How to find a lost pension or 401(k)

Changed jobs a few times? Retirement money is easy to leave behind, especially when a company is sold, merges or closes its plan. Two free federal searches can help you track it down.

Pensions from plans that ended: PBGC

The Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation is the federal agency that backs private-sector pensions. When a plan ends and some people can't be found, PBGC holds their benefits.

  • Search with your last name and the last four digits of your Social Security number on PBGC's unclaimed benefits search.
  • The list is updated every three months.
  • A match isn't proof you're owed money. It's the first step; PBGC confirms the details by phone.

Any old plan: Retirement Savings Lost and Found

The Department of Labor's Retirement Savings Lost and Found shows private-sector employer and union plans linked to your Social Security number, including 401(k)s and pensions, with contact details for each plan.

  • You sign in with a Login.gov account that verifies your identity; have your driver's license and phone ready.
  • It doesn't cover IRAs, government or some religious plans, or Social Security.
  • It shows that you were in a plan, not what's in it. The plan administrator confirms what you're owed.
  • You can only search your own records.

Also worth checking

  • Your state's unclaimed property search, through MissingMoney.com. Small retirement balances are sometimes sent there.
  • Old statements and W-2s, which name the plan or employer to contact.
